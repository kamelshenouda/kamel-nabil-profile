Step 7

Finishing phases, in order

What happens on site, and why each phase comes before the next: messy, wet and hidden work first; finishes, joinery and furniture last.

  1. Approvals and sign-offsCompound approval for the works and the pergola; the structural engineer's sign-off to remove the kitchen walls; the final layout and a contractor quote from the specification list.Before anything is touched
  2. Site set-up, protection and demolitionProtect the entrance and the building lobby, then take down the kitchen walls and the maid's WC walls and clear the rubble.First on site
  3. New walls and openingsBlock walls for the utility, nanny's room and lobby; niches for the TV wall and the key cabinet; lintels and frames for the hidden door and the sliders.Before any pipe or cable goes in
  4. First fix: services inside the walls and floorElectrical conduits and boxes, data, camera and intercom cables to the lobby cabinet; water supply and drains (kitchen, utility, baths, garden); AC copper and condensate lines; the hood duct route.Everything that disappears behind plaster
  5. Plaster, screed and waterproofingWall plaster and corner beads; floor screed to level; waterproofing and flood tests in the bathrooms, the utility and the terrace.Wet trades, done once
  6. Garden hardscapeLevels and drainage, terrace base and paving, deck frames, pergola footings, the outdoor-kitchen base, irrigation sleeves and garden lighting cables. Heavy work now, so it never crosses finished floors.Runs alongside the next phases
  7. Ceilings and ACGypsum ceilings and bulkheads, the LED cove pockets and access hatches; hidden ducted AC units hung and tested before the ceilings close.After first fix, before tiling
  8. Tiling and stoneBathroom walls and floors, the porcelain across the living areas, thresholds and window sills. Floors are protected as soon as they're laid.Hard floors before paint
  9. Doors, windows and glazing framesDoor frames and the flush hidden door; adjust and seal the aluminium sliders; the track for the fluted-glass screen and the oak panel wall.Frames before the last coat of paint
  10. Paint, first coatsFiller, primer and two coats everywhere; ceilings first, then walls.Before wood floors and joinery
  11. Engineered oak floorsLaid in the bedrooms and the office once the wet trades are dry, then covered and protected.After paint, before joinery
  12. Joinery and kitchenKitchen and island, wardrobes, the lobby and utility cabinets, the TV slat wall, the desk and shelves, the oak panel wall and the fluted-glass screen. Worktops are templated on site, then fitted.The longest lead time: order early
  13. Second fix: fittings and appliancesSwitches and sockets, light fittings and LED strips, sanitaryware and taps, the appliances, the Wi-Fi access points, cameras and intercom, and the lighting scenes programmed.Everything you touch
  14. Final paint and touch-upsThe last coat, and touch-ups after the joiners and electricians have finished.After all trades leave
  15. Garden finishing and plantingPergola and outdoor kitchen, the decks, the screen hedges and the planting we design together, then irrigation testing.Plants go in last
  16. Snagging and deep cleanA room-by-room check against the drawings, fixes, then a professional deep clean.Before any furniture arrives
  17. FurnishingCurtain tracks and curtains, rugs, loose furniture, lamps, art and styling; the piano moved in.Deliveries booked around the clean
  18. Move inKeys, manuals and warranties handed over, and the lighting and Wi-Fi set up the way you like them.Welcome home

Order your long-lead items early, while the site is still in its wet phases: kitchen and joinery (6–10 weeks), the fluted glass and panel wall, sanitaryware, the AC units and the engineered oak.
