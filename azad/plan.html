Stage 02 · Garden

The garden: a play lawn and a hosting terrace

The garden faces south-south-east, and an apartment block overlooks it from the west and south-west. So the design puts shade and screening where people sit, and keeps the open lawn for the kids.

  • Pergola terrace (6.86 × 2.40 m, deeper in front of the reception)Porcelain at the same level as inside, under a bronze aluminium louvred pergola. It covers the outdoor lounge in front of the family living room and a dining table for 10 in front of the reception. The louvres block the view down from the apartment block.
  • Three ways to shade itUse the Garden shade switch above the plan, or in 3D. Pergola: the bronze louvred pergola over the terrace, always there, with louvres you can tilt. Motorised shades: two zones, each on its own motor with a remote and a wind sensor. Shade A is a retractable fabric roof on a slim bronze frame over the living and reception terrace, the same footprint as the pergola. Shade B is one cassette awning across the whole bedroom façade, about 7.5 m wide and 3 m deep, over the kids' room and the office. It needs a long-span model with three arms. Roll them out in summer and in to let the winter sun in. None: an open garden, with the shutters doing all the work. On summer middays the terrace is in full sun.
  • Outdoor kitchenA 2.4 m counter with a BBQ, sink and prep space, just outside the pergola so the smoke stays out from under the roof. Useful for the two big feasts a year.
  • Play lawn (9.34 × 3.30 m)Soft lawn outside the kids' room and office, with a timber deck at each sliding door and a playhouse you can see from the island and the family sofa.
  • ScreeningA deep planting bed with a 2.2 m screen on the overlooked west side, a 2 m screen along the south boundary and 1.8 m along the east. The hedges are placeholders for now. We'll choose trees and plants together later.
  • Master courtyardThe side garden becomes a private gravel courtyard with a deck at the master bedroom door, a lounge chair, and a slatted screen from the main garden. Its planting comes later.
  • To confirmCompound rules for the pergola or the motorised shades (shade B fixes to the façade) and the boundary screens, and the garden levels. The terrace sits at floor level, with one step down to the lawn.
Stage 02 · Sun and orientation

The garden faces south-south-east

Measured from the satellite view at 30.0115° N, 31.5091° E: the garden façade faces 155°, turned 25° towards the east from the due south the drawing's arrow suggests. The chart is drawn the same way up as the plans, with the garden at the top. The shaded half of the sky is where the sun reaches the garden glass. The 3D walkthrough now uses this real sun: pick a date and slide through the day.

  • Summer: morning sun, shaded afternoonsOn 21 June the sun is on the garden glass from 06:17 to 13:50, then swings round to the solid west wall. At midday it is almost overhead (83°), and in the morning it comes in at a glancing angle from the east, so little of it gets far into the rooms, even without a balcony above. Facing due south, as drawn, it would have had the sun from 10:11 to 15:41, through the hottest hours.
  • Winter: sun on the glass all dayOn 21 December the sun reaches the garden glass from sunrise to sunset (06:50–16:54), only 37° high at noon. It reaches deep into all three bedrooms and the living room: free warmth in the cool months.
  • Spring and autumnOn 21 March the glass gets sun from 06:01 to 15:07, 60° high at noon.
  • Master bedroom door: early sunThe glazed door to the side courtyard faces south-south-east like the other garden doors. In summer the sun reaches it from about 6:30, and the kids' room shades it after about 1 pm. Its shutter has tight slats for a full blackout.
  • Shutters on every openingAluminium roller shutters with the box hidden in the lintel: motorised on the garden doors, motorised or strap on the small windows. The master-bath window (west-south-west) gets the most summer afternoon sun, about 7 hours on 21 June. The schedule is in the design package, and the 3D model can open, half-close or close them all.
  • The breeze comes from the northCairo's prevailing wind blows from the north and north-west. The service façade with its high windows faces 335°, straight into it, so opening those and the garden doors cross-ventilates the whole apartment.
  • Five floors above youOn summer afternoons the building itself shades the garden. On 21 June the lawn outside the kids' room goes into its shade at about 14:39 and the terrace outside the living room at about 15:40; on 21 March the lawn at 16:25. In winter the sun stays on the garden side all day, so the building never shades the garden. The 3D model now includes these floors (they cast shade but aren't drawn, so you can still see in).
  • Not in the model yetThe rest of the block on either side of your apartment, which brings the afternoon shade earlier on the reception side, and the buildings across the garden. In winter the sun is low in the south, only 37° at noon, so a six-storey block within about 25 m to the south would shade the garden and the glass.

What the site video showed me

  • Walls are cement-block infill set in a concrete frame. That's good news for opening up the kitchen. It still needs the engineer's sign-off.
  • The ceilings are a flat ribbed slab with no beams crossing the rooms. There are deep beams only over the big garden openings. So coves and ducted AC can run cleanly.
  • The garden openings have a raised concrete edge, and the garden sits a little lower than the floor. We'll design flush thresholds with a drainage channel and one outdoor step.
  • Apartment blocks overlook the garden from the west and south-west. The garden needs a pergola and tall screen planting on that side.
  • There's a service shaft in the maid and laundry area, where the utility sink, dishwasher and washer can drain.

I can see the video, but I can't hear the narration. If you said anything important in it, please send it as a short text.
